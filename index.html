Prologue

Why I'm writing this

Many people believe a career is decided in their twenties. Mine has been rebuilt more than once: from a technical diploma to the sales floor, from leading a department of 80 people to starting an online business from nothing, and then, at an age when most people stop changing direction, into banking.

I'm sharing it for colleagues who are thinking about a change of their own, or who wonder whether the experience they have counts for anything in a new field. In my experience, it counts for a great deal.

The journey

Seven chapters

Each one taught something the next one needed.

  1. 01
    Mumbai & Pune · Before 2002

    Foundations

    Rosary High School, Mumbai · Diploma in Air-Conditioning & Refrigeration, Kohinoor Technical Institute, Pune

    My first training was technical: how systems work, why they fail and how to fix them. I didn't stay in engineering, but the habit stayed with me. Before I try to solve a customer's problem, I want to understand how it works.

    Lesson

    Understand the system before you try to fix it.

    Learning
  2. 02
    2002 – 2008 · Doha & Abu Dhabi

    The shop floor

    Lulu Hypermarket / Center · Sales Representative → Supervisor → Sales Manager

    I started as a sales representative and grew into supervision and sales management. By the end, I was generating around AED 300K in daily revenue through campaigns, brand partnerships and large events. The floor teaches you quickly that every sale starts with listening.

    Lesson

    The customer always tells you what they need. You just have to be listening.

    ServiceTrust
  3. 03
    2008 – 2011 · Dubai

    Learning the business side

    Eros Electricals / Digital Home · Corporate Sales B2B Consultant / Showroom Manager

    Selling to hotels, real estate developers and government projects meant tenders, contracts and collections. It was my first real lesson that a sale isn't finished until the money is collected and the client wants to come back.

    Lesson

    A sale is only complete when the relationship continues.

    TrustLearning
  4. 04
    2011 – 2018 · Al Khor, Qatar

    Leading people

    Lulu Hypermarket, Al Khor Mall · Department Store Manager

    Seven years running Electronics, IT and Lifestyle: 80 team members, around AED 500K in daily sales, and full responsibility for P&L, CAPEX and OPEX. My proudest results weren't the sales figures. They were the people I coached who went on to grow.

    Lesson

    Results come from people. Invest in them and the numbers follow.

    ServiceTrust
  5. 05
    2019 – 2024 · Dubai

    Reinvention

    Fuyu General Trading · Sales Manager  →  88 Star Trading · Operations Manager

    After seven years in Al Khor I returned to Dubai and went back into the field: market surveys, outdoor sales, studying the competition. In 2020 I built an e-commerce division from scratch and led a 12-member team to around AED 5.5 million in annual sales, with growth every year.

    Lesson

    When the market moves, move with it. Experience is something you carry with you into each new role.

    LearningService
  6. 06
    2024 – 2026 · Dubai

    The leap into banking

    Commercial Bank of Dubai · Sales Officer – Retention Agent

    In my mid-forties I started again, in a new industry with new products, regulations and vocabulary. On the retention desk I helped credit card customers who were about to leave, matching Cash on Call, Balance Transfer and Easy Payment Plans to their real needs. The products were new to me, but customers' reasons for staying or leaving hadn't changed.

    Lesson

    Starting again is uncomfortable. It is also where most growth happens.

    TrustLearning
  7. 07
    July 2026 – Present · Dubai

    Building trust that lasts

    Dubai Islamic Bank · Retention Officer, Personal Banking

    Today I work to keep salary-transfer and deposit customers with the bank, using CASA accounts, Sharia-compliant Wakala deposits, remittances and Walaa Rewards. I build my own tools too: a daily retention tracker, a product guide that checks itself every week, and training material for new officers.

    Lesson

    Retention is really service, done consistently over time.

For anyone starting over

Notes I would give my younger self

These are drawn from my own path, and from what others who moved from retail into finance say helped them.

Your experience is not wasted

Listening, leading and closing work in every industry. Translate your experience into the new field's language instead of starting from zero.

Talk to people already there

The fastest way to understand a new industry is to ask people who work in it. Most are happy to help.

Plan the leap, then take it

Picture the worst case. If you can live with it, the fear gets smaller. Prepare well, then commit.

Never stop learning

At 47 I am still collecting certificates in Excel, Power BI, risk and AI. Curiosity doesn't retire.

Beyond the job title

A life across cultures

I went to school in Mumbai, studied in Pune, built my career across Qatar and the UAE, and have made the UAE my home. Working with colleagues and customers from every corner of the world taught me that respect and patience are understood in every language.

Today I live in Sharjah with my family. As a father of two sons, I want my own path to show them that it's never too late to learn something new or begin again.
